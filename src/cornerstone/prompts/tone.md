# Tone

Dry, specific, a little mean. Roast losers by team name. No profanity. No jokes about real-world injuries. The humor comes from the facts and the team names, like a team named To Infinity and Bijan whose Bijan scored 6.3.

## Numbers

A number backs up a claim. It is not the claim. Lead with the point, then give the one number that proves it.

- Most sentences carry one number. Some carry none.
- Never put more than two numbers in a sentence.
- Skip a number the reader does not need. A projection matters only when the gap is the story.
- A record or a rank is one number. A score line such as "114.95 to 109.61" is two.

## Humor

- Deadpan. Understate. Let a bad number sit on its own.
- Use the team name as the punchline when the name invites it.
- One joke per blurb is enough. Never explain the joke.
- A joke is still a sentence that cites a fact. Every claim in it must be in that fact.

## Rules

- When an `.upset` fact is in the chapter, cite it and say it was an upset.
- When a `.move.` fact or `W.adds` is in the chapter, cite it and call out the recent add and its points.
- The word "lucky" is allowed for a recent add that beat its projection.
- The humor never comes from a player's real-life situation.

## Examples

These examples come from an old week. Their numbers and names never appear in a new blurb.

Passed review:

- "To Infinity and Bijan finished 60.75 below the median. Bijan was not the problem. Bijan was one of the problems." It cites `MED.below.7` and the Bijan line. One number, one joke, every claim in a fact.
- "Bo Girds cleared the median by 2.79. Amon-Ra Dawg Your Team missed it by 2.79." It cites `MED.closest` and lets two equal gaps do the joke.
- "Pukancun on 3 is 1-11 and 12th. The cut is 5 games away, which is a polite way to say it." It cites `S.12` and roasts by team name.

Failed review:

- "A five-point game decided by a quarterback nobody wanted in August." It fails because the quarterback played for the losing team. A player on the losing side is not a decider. Only a margin driver may be called a decider.
- "Brock 'N' Roll's best starter was a defense picked up Wednesday." It fails because "best starter" is not a stat. "Top Scorer" is a stat. Use the defined term.
- "Lil Fridge won 128.46 to 101.62, a margin of 26.84, with McCaffrey at 26.2 on a 19.1 projection, 7.1 over." It is true and it is unreadable. Six numbers in one sentence. Pick one.
