# A key starter reads real starts, not the best lineup

The Next Week preview names the players on bye next week and roasts the hit a team takes. The rubric and the chart rank every player by lineup value and grade a roster by its best lineup, so the obvious rule for a key starter is "in the best lineup most weeks". We chose the other rule: a key starter is a player the team started in at least 3 of its last 4 completed weeks, read from the saved matchups. The roast is about the manager, and the best lineup says what he should have done. Real starts say what he did. A manager who benched a top receiver for four weeks and now loses him to a bye is the joke, and the best lineup cannot see it.

## Consequences

- The saved history must keep each team's starters per week. The matchups file does, so no new fetch is needed.
- A player added this week has no starts on his new roster and is never a key starter, even when he is a star. The names line still shows him on bye.
- A start in the FLEX slot counts. A kicker or a defense never counts, whatever his starts.
- With fewer than 4 played weeks the bar drops to 2 of 3, then 2 of 2, so week 2 and week 3 have key starters too.
- The best lineup still picks the bye cover. The cover is about the roster, not about the manager's habit.
- The trade analyzer's star rule is a chart rank and does not change. The two terms are distinct in the glossary.
