# Vocabulary

These terms define the stats. Each term means exactly what its line says. When you name a stat, use its term. A plain phrase that the cited fact supports is fine. Never use a word that names a different stat.

A "Plain phrase" on a line is an example of the plain phrase for that term. Prefer it to the term. A plain phrase needs the same cited fact as its term.

- **Top Scorer**: the starter with the most points. League-wide, it is the starter with the most points on any team.
- **MVB**: the bench player with the most points, across all teams.
- **Underperformer**: the starter with the largest gap below projection, among starters projected at 10 or more.
- **Overachiever**: the starter with the largest gap above projection.
- **Unlucky**: the starter with the largest gap below his expected points, at least 5 under. Expected points are what his volume should have scored. Say "unlucky" only in a sentence that cites `AW.unlucky`.
- **Unlucky award in prose**: name it "the Unlucky award", never a bare "Unlucky". In the same sentence, say what it measures, with the numbers as the fact gives them. Plain phrase: "took the Unlucky award: 6.1 points from a stat line that usually scores about 14.8".
- **Lucky**: the starter with the largest gap above his expected points, at least 5 over. Say "lucky" for it only in a sentence that cites `AW.lucky`.
- **Lucky award in prose**: name it "the Lucky award", never a bare "Lucky". In the same sentence, say what it measures, with the numbers as the fact gives them. Plain phrase: "took the Lucky award: 22.7 points from a stat line that usually scores about 12.49".
- **Game Changer**: the starter on a winning side whose points exceed the margin, ranked by gap to projection, with a bonus for a recent add.
- **Matchup of the Week**: the matchup that scored highest on closest margin, biggest upset, highest combined standing, and nearness to the playoff cut.
- **margin driver**: a player on either side whose gap to projection, in absolute terms, is at least the margin. Only a margin driver may be called a decider. Plain phrase for "margin driver" or "decider": "won it" on the winning side, "blew it" on the losing side.
- **swing player**: a player on the winning side whose points exceed the margin. Say "without X, that is a loss" only for a swing player. Plain phrase: "outscored the margin on his own".
- **bench regret**: a bench player who, swapped for the lowest starter at the same position, would have flipped the result.
- **Must Watch**: one of the two next-week games with the highest storyline score. A storyline is a streak on the line, a shootout of top-four projected lineups, a rematch, revenge, near the cut, or the top of the table.
- **next opponent win rate**: the win rate of a team's opponent next week, with the league rank of that number, toughest first. It covers next week only, not the rest of the season. Plain phrase: "a hard draw next week".
- **position rank**: a starter's rank among all starters at the same position this week.
- **position leader**: one of the three started players with the most points at one position this week, league wide.
- **streak**: head-to-head results only, the median game excluded.
- **team trend**: the last 3 weeks against the season average.
- **player trend**: against projection over the last 3 weeks.
- **season high**: a team's highest weekly total this season.
- **season low**: a team's lowest weekly total this season.
- **season average**: a team's points for divided by the weeks played this season.
- **points per game**: half-PPR points over the weeks with a scored game, weeks 1 to N-1.
- **position rank by season points**: among every player at the position in Sleeper's weekly stats, weeks 1 to N-1.
- **upset**: the winner ranked below the loser in the standings before this week. Use it only in a sentence that cites a fact with `.upset` in its ID.
- **recent add**: a starter added by waiver, free agent, or trade in this week or the week before. Use it only in a sentence that cites a fact with `.move.` in its ID or the fact `W.adds`. Say "lucky" only in a sentence that cites a fact with `.move.` in its ID, the fact `W.adds`, or the fact `AW.lucky`. Say "lucky" at most once per page, and only for a recent add that scored at least twice its projection or for the Lucky award. Plain phrase: "the new pickup".
- **injury status**: the Sleeper designation for a player as of the Tuesday after the games. It says nothing about the player's status at kickoff. Use it only in a sentence that cites a fact with `.inj.` in its ID or the fact `W.injured`.
- **injury history**: the weekly NFL report status from nflverse, per week.
- **pre-game status**: the final NFL report before the game, from nflverse. A roast of a manager who started a player listed Out or Doubtful before the game cites this fact.
- **drop**: a pass the FTN charting flags as a drop. With nflverse down, the count is Sleeper's `rec_drop`.
- **drop value**: an estimate. One reception at the league rate, plus the air yards at the per-yard rate, plus a touchdown when the throw reached the end zone.
- **drop swing**: the losing side's drop value is at least the margin. Only then may the writer say the drops cost the game.
- **WOPR**: weighted opportunity rating, 1.5 times target share plus 0.7 times air yards share; a weighted sum and not a share, so never a percent; it can pass 100 or go negative.
- **WOPR in prose**: never write "WOPR" in prose. Say what it measures. Plain phrase: "the 2nd-biggest share of his team's targets and air yards among started tight ends", or shorter, "2nd in passing volume among started TEs".
- **expected points**: what his volume should have scored. Never write "expected points" in prose. Plain phrase: "what his stat line usually scores".
- **carry share**: his share of his team's carries, in percent.

## Banned words

Never write these words: best, star, carried, hero, MVP, clutch, exploded, cooked, WOPR.

## Banned phrases

Never write these phrases: the entire point, one of the problems, a lot to hang on, a polite way to say, expected points.
