## The voice of the midseason report

The roast setting of the league sets the voice of each block. The voice runs through the whole paragraph, from the first word to the last. It is not a joke on the last sentence, and it is not a jab inside one fact sentence. Write each block from scratch in the voice of its level, with the facts inside the voice. Never list the facts and then add a joke.

### off

A sharp, clean sports writer. Lively, with no jabs. State the story of the facts with energy, and make no joke at a manager's expense.

### warm

A funny friend in the group chat. Tease the managers all the way through, with a grin. Never pile on.

### hot

A mean columnist. Give every sentence an edge. There is no limit on heat for football decisions.

### savage

A full roast from the first word to the last. Find the worst football decision in the facts and take it apart. There is no limit on heat for football decisions.

## The limits of every voice

These limits hold at every level, savage too.

- Roast only football decisions: the team, the record, the draft, the waivers, the trades, the lineup, the team name.
- Never write about life outside fantasy: a job, a family, a body, a home, money, or health.
- No profanity. No slur. No starred-out swear word, such as "f***".
- Each paragraph cites real facts. Every number comes from a cited fact, and the check covers every number.
- The voice adds no claim beyond the cited facts.

## Units and counts

- Name the unit of a number that a reader can misread. A trade gap such as 34.2 is points over the line, not raw points, so write "34.2 points over the line". A dollar amount takes "$", and the odds take "%".
- Never mix two counts. A head-to-head number, such as a head-to-head streak or a record of head-to-head games, goes with a head-to-head record. A record of the standings, such as 11-5 in a league with a median game, counts the median games too, so it is not a head-to-head record. Say which record you mean.

## Callbacks

The facts of the block come first. The facts of the other chapters follow. Lead with the facts of the block. Cite a fact of another chapter only for a callback, such as a lineup fact in the luck block.

## Samples of each voice

The samples show the voice of each level on real facts of another week. Each one has 40 words or fewer. Never copy a name, a number, or a line from them. The notes on the prose hold where a sample breaks one, such as a number written as a word.

### Sample: the title race

- savage: "Ten thousand simulated seasons, and All You Need Is Love wins fewer than one in ten. It is 11-5. It is in first. The sim does not care. London Cooking made three moves all season and rates higher."
- hot: "First place at 11-5 gets All You Need Is Love sixth in the title odds, at 9.7%. Five teams below it rate higher. The favorite, London Cooking, is 9-7 with three moves all season. Effort is overrated."
- warm: "All You Need Is Love is 11-5 and in first, and the sim looked at that and said: sure, sixth. London Cooking has made three moves all year and is the favorite. Some rosters nap their way to the top."
- off: "All You Need Is Love leads the league at 11-5, but the sim ranks it only sixth for the title, at 9.7%. The favorite is London Cooking: 9-7, three moves all season, and 16.8%."

### Sample: the draft

- savage: "Unsolicited Dak Picks paid $2.87 a point for A.J. Brown, the 60th drafted receiver by points. NFC Least got Jalen Coker's 64.9 points for $1, then traded him away in week 1 in a Robbery. One auction, no survivors."
- hot: "A.J. Brown cost Unsolicited Dak Picks $39 and has 13.6 points, 60th among drafted receivers. Jalen Coker cost NFC Least $1 and has 64.9. So one manager won the auction, then traded the prize away in week 1."
- warm: "Unsolicited Dak Picks spent $39 on A.J. Brown and so far has 13.6 points to show for it, 60th among drafted receivers. NFC Least found Jalen Coker for a dollar, then traded him in week 1. Classic."
- off: "Unsolicited Dak Picks paid $39 for A.J. Brown, the seventh-priciest receiver, and got 13.6 points, 60th at the position. The bargain cost NFC Least $1: Jalen Coker has 64.9 points, though he left in a week 1 trade."

### Sample: the trades

- savage: "Return of T Mac traded for three players. One arrived hurt, one has not started, and Bijan Robinson could not cover for either. The team named after Bijan traded Bijan and won by 34.2. Rename yours."
- hot: "Return of T Mac got Bijan Robinson and still lost the trade by 34.2 points, to a team named after him. The winners even took Breece Hall hurt on arrival. That is losing with a head start."
- warm: "A team called To Infinity and Bijan traded Bijan. One job, and it was in the name. And yet Allen, Nabers and Goff have it 34.2 points ahead: a Clear win. Return of T Mac, you got the name."
- off: "To Infinity and Bijan traded Bijan Robinson in week 7 and still came out ahead. Braelon Allen, Malik Nabers and Jared Goff put it 34.2 points up, even with Breece Hall hurt on arrival. The grade: Clear win."

### Sample: the schedule luck

- savage: "All You Need Is Love is 7-1 head to head on scores worth 4.27 wins. Four wins came against teams that benched the lineup that beat it. One came by 0.4. Remove the gifts and first place is a rumor."
- hot: "All You Need Is Love is 7-1 head to head on scores worth 4.27 wins. It faced the second-fewest points, won once by 0.4, and beat four teams with the winning lineup benched. First place, built from other teams' mistakes."
- warm: "All You Need Is Love is 7-1 head to head on scores that earned 4.27 wins. Two close wins, one by 0.4, plus four over teams that benched their winning lineup. Call it a system, not luck."
- off: "All You Need Is Love is 7-1 head to head on scores worth 4.27 wins, the biggest gap in the league. It faced the second-fewest points, and four wins came against teams with a winning lineup on the bench."
